# RecallGraph art direction: Checkpoint A

## Signature element

**Closure stitch:** One continuous lineage ribbon begins visibly open, briefly forks to show a contained downstream consequence, then reconverges. A deliberate, transverse stitch crosses the reconverged ribbon and closes it into a sealed terminal. The movement is from exposed lineage to defensible closure, not a static graph. The fork is evidence of impact, and the thicker stitch is the human-governed act that makes closure credible.

## Material language

Precise flat vector craft: optically corrected monoline geometry for the ribbon and one visibly thicker transverse stitch. Vermilion #F05A3C is reserved for that closure action; graphite #111718, warm bone #F4F0E8, and muted steel #7C8788 provide contrast and field. No glass, 3D, shading, raster texture, or effect-driven depth.

## Composition

Center the mark as one strong silhouette with generous negative space. Keep the ribbon’s open-to-sealed progression immediately legible; permit asymmetry only where the stitch creates tension. The vector route must remain clean at the 16px floor for a web logomark and favicon, while retaining a measured fork-and-reconvergence reading at larger sizes.

## Forbidden

Reject the obvious literal solution: a generic node-link network or graph. Also forbid generic network nodes, magnifying glass, shield, padlock, checkmark badge, warning triangle, database cylinder, chain link, brain/circuit, robot, sparkles, glowing orb, hexagon, rainbow, gradients that introduce another hue, text/letters/numbers inside the icon, trademarks, busy composition, unrelated stacked motifs, and any silhouette that reads as an R. Do not substitute a camera, eye, key, fingerprint, gear, or AI/crypto cliché for the closure stitch.

## Dual-legibility target

At 16px, read instantly as a single intentional open-to-sealed ribbon with one decisive stitch. At 512px, reward inspection with the brief downstream fork, precise reconvergence, and the contrast between exposed lineage and defensible, human-governed closure.
